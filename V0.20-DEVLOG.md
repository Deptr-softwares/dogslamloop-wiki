## Built 2026-09-24: the three bugs, and FT2

### Bug 1: a Combo Card named after the switch was still "New Combo"

Two faults behind one symptom, and only the reproduction found the second.

**The card list read `card.title` directly.** That is dead data once Multiple
Sections is on, because `description.js` renders a tab per section and each
section carries its own heading, so the card's own title reaches no reader.

**`seedCardSections` copied the card's title into the section's TAB LABEL.** For
a card switched on before it was named that is the template default, so "New
Combo" was planted as a genuine label and renaming never cleared it. Readers saw
it on the tab. The label is seeded EMPTY now: it is an optional override for the
case the owner's reference shows, a tab reading "In Corner 6H" over a much
longer heading, and the reader already falls back to the title when it is blank.

`window.comboCardLabel` reads TITLE before label, the opposite of the reader's
tab row, because it is naming a card rather than a tab. It reads the FIRST
section rather than the open one, because a reader lands on tab 0 and using the
open tab would rename the card every time one was clicked.

### Bug 2: the Combos preview snapping back to the first tab

**Found only because the owner sent a screen recording.** Three written
descriptions and four probes had all aimed at the editor form. It is the
PREVIEW: the form edits section 2, the author types one line into Route, and the
preview jumps to section 1.

**The cause is the shape of the v0.19 fix, not a new bug.** v0.19 solved this
and solved it inline in `populateTextSection`. `renderDocumentTab` is a second
repaint path, clears its container the same way, and never got it, so the
Section Box was fixed everywhere except the two tabs a Combo Card lives in.

Capture and restore are now `window.captureNestedViewState` and
`window.restoreNestedViewState`, called from both. Still matched BY POSITION:
the title is what is being typed when somebody renames a section.

### Bug 3: CLOSED, not reproducible, and NOT verified fixed

The owner could not reproduce it again and asked to drop it (2026-09-24).
Recorded rather than deleted, because closing it and fixing it are different
things and a future session should not read this as verified.

**What was probed and came back clean:** a Combo Card nested in a Section Box

**A plausible but UNPROVEN explanation:** bug 2 would look exactly like this.

through huge amount of content is easier than navigating through a bunch of

## Built 2026-09-24: Clash notices on the Matchup Chart

The Matchup Chart now points out every pair where the two character pages
disagree, and lists them under the chart, biggest disagreement first.

### The rule, chosen by the owner

**A Clash is a pair whose two pages don't mirror each other**: Advantage on one
side should read Disadvantage on the other, Equal should read Equal. Their
example: *"Honored One vs True Cannon is Equal but True Cannon vs Honored One is
Hopeless, that's a clash."*

How strict was put to them with the counts read off production (178 pairs rated
from both sides):

| Rule | Pairs flagged |
|---|---|
| **Any mismatch, one step included (CHOSEN)** | **96** |
| Disagree on who wins | 86 |
| Three or more steps apart | 46 |

So Advantage against Extreme Disadvantage is a Clash, even though both pages
agree who wins. That pair is in the fixture as the assertion that pins the
decision. **Do not loosen the rule to shorten the list.**

The mirror is computed off `window.MATCHUP_TIERS`, not named tiers: a rung and
its mirror always sum to the last index. Ratings go through
`resolveMatchupTier` first, so the two v0.13 renames compare as the words they
became.

**Never a Clash:** a pair rated from one side only (nothing to disagree with),
and wording off the ladder (`"Aerial Circling tier"` has no mirror to compare,
and guessing one is the guess `resolveMatchupTier` already refuses to make).

**Marked, never resolved.** The grid still shows both ratings exactly as
written. v0.19 recorded that the chart must never be made symmetrical, and a
Clash notice is that same rule made visible rather than an exception to it.

### What a reader sees

* **A dark corner on both cells of the pair.** Fixed `#111`, not a theme token,
  because the tier colours under it are fixed and every one is light enough.
* **The cell's tooltip names the other side** on a second line: *"Clash: Honored
  One vs Restless Gambler is Equal"*. A screen reader gets it as a sentence.
* **A "Clashes (96)" list under the legend**, collapsed, one notice per pair in
  the owner's own sentence shape, each half linking to the page that wrote it.
  Biggest gap first, then roster order. A **Clash** legend entry sits beside the
  tiers. With no Clashes, neither the list nor the legend entry exists. The
  list's explanation line is the owner's own wording, set in review on #204.
* **Hidden characters are not in the chart at all** (owner, in review on #204):
  Strongest of History, private server only, `isHidden` in `navigation.json`.
  Filtered where the roster is read, so it is gone as a row, as a column and
  from every Clash in one place.

### Verified against production, 2026-09-24

96 notices and 192 marked cells, matching the independent count. Every marked
cell's mirror is marked. The owner's example renders word for word. Top of the
list, both six steps off: Defense Attorney and True Cannon **both claim the
win**, Head of the Hei and True Cannon **both claim the loss**.

**82 of the 96 have Equal on one side.** Many of those are likely a default
nobody changed rather than a considered rating, so the list doubles as a to-do
list for the matchup pages. That is content, and the owner's.

Falsified on committed code, six mutations, each failing the tests it should:
the feature absent (7 fail), alias resolution skipped (3), the "who wins" rule
the owner turned down (4), no sort (2), one-sided pairs judged (3), off-ladder
wording judged (4).

### Bug found on the way: the hub scrolled sideways on phones

**Live since v0.19.** At a 390px viewport `systems/index.html` was 639px wide.
The grid's wrapper does scroll, but every cell carries an absolutely positioned
`.sr-only` label, and those resolved against an ancestor OUTSIDE the wrapper, so
they escaped its clipping and widened the page. Hiding the labels or making the
wrapper `position: relative` each brought it back to 390; the second is the fix.

A clashing link is itself `position: relative`, which would have hidden this on
exactly the cells Clashes touch, so the regression test uses a 24-character
roster with no Clash in it. Without the fix it reads 620 against 390.

## Built 2026-09-25: a search hit lands on its section

Opening a search result for a section low on a page now lands on that section,
even when the page is still loading around it.

### The report, in the owner's words

> *"Searching for sections that is lower in a page sometimes won't auto-scroll
> you to it. For example, I search for the move "Murmurate" in Crow Charmer page
> in the Main Dashboard, but the auto-scroll stopped at Circling. But if I
> search for Murmurate when I am in Crow Charmer page, it goes to the right
> place."*

### Two faults, both in the arrival code (`js/pagebuilder.js`)

**Measured on the real page, not reasoned about.** With the page's data or
media held back by 2 to 2.5s, it stopped at `sec-use-cases`, a subsection of
Circling, with Murmurate 2,684px below the viewport: the owner's symptom
exactly.

1. **It scrolled once, at a target that then moved.** Murmurate first existed
   while the Skills tab was half built, at 4,035px on a 6,662px page. The one
   retry that found it fired a smooth scroll there, and 180ms later the skills
   above it finished rendering and pushed it to 6,679px. The scroll finished at
   the stale position. From the page itself the tab is already built, which is
   why it only failed on arrival.
2. **It stopped looking 2.5s after DOMContentLoaded.** On a connection where the
   content took longer, the last retry ran before the section existed and the
   page never moved at all. Reproduced with a 2s data delay on a direct link.

### The fix: look, land, then follow

* **Look** for up to 15s instead of 2.5s.
* **Land instantly**, never smoothly. A smooth scroll aimed at a moving target
  is the bug itself, and an instant jump is what a browser does with a fragment
  on load anyway.
* **Follow** for up to 10s: whenever content above the section grows, move with
  it. A heading replaced by a re-render is followed by id, not by node.
* **The reader wins.** Their first wheel, key, click or touch ends the follow,
  and so does any scroll that did not come from the follower (a scrollbar drag
  fires no input event). A page that pulls somebody back after they started
  reading elsewhere is worse than one that lands short.
* **`#post-` is left to `js/discussions.js`**, which centres a reply rather than
  topping it. Following those too would fight its scroll.

Chromium's own scroll anchoring would cover some of the follow on its own, so
one test turns anchoring off to prove the follower does the work in a browser
without it.

### The old arrival test never tested arrival

It navigated to the page, read a section id, then went to `PAGE#id`. That only
changes the fragment, which is a **same-document navigation**: no reload, no
network, no arrival code. It exercised the `hashchange` handler on a page that
was already built, the one path that always worked. It also only asserted
`scrollY > 100`, which the bug passes (it stopped at 3,995). The helper now
leaves the page before arriving, and the assertion is that the section sits
near the top of the viewport.

**Two traps in writing the tests, both worth keeping:**
* A `scrollTo` wrapper that forwards `(opts, y)` calls `scrollTo(opts,
  undefined)`, which the browser reads as `scrollTo(x, y)` and sends to 0,0.
  My first probe of this bug had that wrapper, and its "the smooth scroll had
  not started" reading was the wrapper, not the page. Forward `...args`.
* Reading a deep section once after `networkidle` came back empty once in six
  runs under four workers, and `test.skip` turned that into a silent pass. It
  waits now.

Falsified on committed code: the old `pagebuilder.js` fails 3 of the 5 arrival
tests; landing without following fails 2; never releasing the reader fails 1;
the old 2.5s budget fails 1. The links spec passes 54 of 54 at three repeats
under four workers.

**A flake met on the way, and not this change's:** `page-skeleton-parity`'s
"skeleton contract" asserts at DOMContentLoaded that the tabs are not built yet,
which races the content fetch. Measured at three repeats: 1 in 96 fails on
`next-update`'s own `pagebuilder.js`, 3 in 96 with this change, which does
nothing at all on a page without a hash. A different page fails each time.
Worth making deterministic (hold the fetch with a route) before it costs a CI
run.

## SPEC 2026-09-25: notation styles on combo routes

### The owner's words

> *"Combo Block, Combo Card, and Combo Row now have the options to be toggled
> between notation styles: On the reader side, the combo block, combo card +
> combo row line will have a small toggle visuals on the side, which is very
> small and thin; reader switch styles by clicking on the combo text itself; On
> the editor side, it will be an optional things to give to a Combo Block,
> Combo Card's line and Combo Row's line, and by creating new style option, they
> have to fill in a new field."*

Two questions put to them, 2026-09-25:

| Question | Their answer |
|---|---|
| How is a style named? | **The author types a label** (over a fixed site-wide list, and over an unnamed flip) |
| What does a reader's click switch? | **Every combo, remembered** on that device (over "only that combo") |

### What production holds, read 2026-09-25

83 Combo Blocks, 11 Combo Cards (2 in Multiple Sections), 24 Combo Rows, every
one with exactly one route. Routes are mostly move names (`Gushing Wound >
Homerun`) with some slot shorthand (`S1 DETONATE`, `ROOT SWARM (S1)`, `R↑`). The
Combo Row's `controls` column exists and is empty on the one row that has the
key. Nothing resembling a second notation exists yet.

### Data shape

```js
// Beside every `sequence`: a Combo Block, a Combo Card, each section of a
// Combo Card in Multiple Sections, and a Combo Row.
notations: [{ label: 'Keyboard', sequence: ['Q', 'E', 'R'] }]
```

* **The existing `sequence` stays the first style**, unlabelled. Additive: every
  stored combo, and every old revision replayed from history, renders exactly
  as it does today.
* **A style needs a label AND at least one step to render.** A half-filled one
  is an author mid-edit, skipped by the reader rather than shown as an empty
  route.

### Reader

* **A combo with no styles renders byte-identical markup to today.**
* With styles: every style's route is in the DOM, one visible. The route row
  gets a thin mark at its start, one segment per style, the active one lit. The
  route itself is the control: click, Enter or Space moves to the next style
  and wraps back to the first.
* **The choice is a LABEL, and it applies to every combo.** Labels match
  ignoring case and extra spaces, so "Keyboard" and "keyboard " are one style.
  A combo with a style under that label shows it; one without shows its first
  route. Remembered in `localStorage` (a per-viewer convenience, wrapped in
  try/catch, the page works without it).
* Tooltip and screen-reader label: *"Notation 2 of 3: Keyboard. Click the route
  to switch."* The first style has no label, so it reads *"Notation 1 of 3"*.
* One shared route renderer replaces the three copies of the chip loop (Combo
  Block, Combo Card, Combo Row).

### Editor

* **Four surfaces, one helper.** The Combo Block form, the Combo Card block form,
  the Combos/Techs card editor, and the Combo Row modal. The Combo Card has
  drifted between its two editors three times; the style rows, their handlers
  and their rules are written once and called from all four.
* "+ ADD NOTATION STYLE" adds a row with two fields, a **style name** and a
  **route**, and focuses the name. The route field follows its surface's own
  convention: comma separated on the Combo Block, one step per line everywhere
  else.
* **`seedCardSections` carries the styles** when Multiple Sections is switched on
  or off, exactly like `sequence`, or they would be stranded behind the switch.

### Reviewer

`admin-preview.js` diffs every route step for all three surfaces. It diffs every
style's label and steps too, and in the review view **every style is shown at
once, each labelled**: a reviewer must never have to click to find a changed
route.
